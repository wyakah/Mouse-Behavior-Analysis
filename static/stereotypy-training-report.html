What was trained, and what still needs validation
  • Learned: a temporal network and six output heads, with a denoising reconstruction adapter. ImageNet MobileNetV3 image weights stayed frozen. This is a video-classifier experiment, not a DeepLabCut pose fine-tune.
  • Grooming and rearing: MIT human annotations. Its original ethogram is mutually exclusive; our assay may allow overlapping behaviors, so the label mapping needs local verification.
  • Digging: human annotations from the OSF pup-retrieval study. Its overhead view and maternal context differ from your side-view cage.
  • Nonfood gnawing, jumping and circling: one Stanford positive demonstration video each, trained as whole-video bags. Individual windows were not declared positive. Resting examples from MIT supply explicitly derived negatives. The model can learn recording backgrounds instead of behavior; independent accuracy is unknown. The circling source definition is broader than our completed-turn definition.
  • Your recordings: cage-cropped, unlabeled reconstruction training and feature normalization. No manual behavior labels were invented, and these two mice are not an independent accuracy test.
  • Evaluation: whole source groups held out. Preprocessing statistics and weight updates exclude external validation and test data. Epoch selection uses validation average precision; test scores are reported at a fixed 0.5 threshold.
  • Next acceptance step: verify local positive and negative clips for each behavior, add independent mice and multiple positive recordings for the rare classes, then measure per-class precision/recall, duration error and event-count error. Human-reviewed labels remain authoritative.

Sources: MIT mouse behavior dataset · Winters et al. annotated digging data · Stanford Mouse Ethogram. Data and model weights remain local; redistribution rights were not established.
